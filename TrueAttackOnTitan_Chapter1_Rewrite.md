# True Attack on Titan: Beautiful World
## 真・進撃の巨人

---

**Summary:** After Eren reached the ocean, he was disappointed in himself. After all, he chose to see the outside world instead of saving his mother. This is the story of how Eren becomes free—from self-hatred, from toxic bonds, from fate itself.

---

# Chapter One: Fly Upon the Sky

*In which Gabi Braun learns why she fights*

---

The salt spray stung Floch's wound as he dragged himself over the ship's railing.

His shoulder screamed—a white-hot agony that pulsed with each heartbeat—but he had stopped caring about pain somewhere between the beach and the open water. He collapsed onto the deck, chest heaving, and stared up at the gray sky as consciousness flickered like a dying candle.

*How did it come to this?*

The question pulled him backward, through blood and gunfire and desperate swimming, until he found himself standing in a memory that still felt like a splinter lodged beneath his skin.

---

**Three Months Earlier**

The farmhouse sat at the edge of Historia's lands, far from prying eyes. Floch had received the Queen's summons with the same hollow indifference he brought to everything these days—the meetings, the patrols, the endless pretense that any of it mattered.

He knew the truth now. They all did. Beyond the walls, beyond the ocean, an entire world wanted them dead. Fifty years, the military brass said. Fifty years to prepare. Fifty years of borrowed time before annihilation came knocking.

Floch had stopped believing in time.

He pushed open the farmhouse door expecting another pointless briefing, another exercise in futility dressed up as strategy. What he found instead was Eren Yeager sitting alone at a rough wooden table, his green eyes catching the lamplight like something feral.

The silence between them stretched taut as wire.

Floch remained standing. He had nothing to say to the man who had begged—*begged*—for Armin Arlert's life while Commander Erwin Smith bled out in the ruins of Shiganshina. That choice had hollowed something out of Floch, carved away whatever capacity he'd once had for faith in others.

"Our relationship didn't work out," Eren said finally. His voice was flat, stripped of the passion Floch remembered from their training days. "You don't want to talk to me. I understand that. But I'm here to fix it."

Eren's gaze drifted toward the door—toward where Historia surely waited, listening.

"Fix it?" Floch let out a sound that might have been a laugh if it had any warmth in it. "Don't make me laugh. Say what you came to say, Yeager. I have better things to do than sit here pretending we're comrades."

The words landed like stones, but Eren didn't flinch. He simply watched Floch with those unsettling eyes—older than they should be, weighted with something Floch couldn't name.

"It's about Historia's protection."

Floch's jaw tightened. "What about it?"

"Our government has betrayed us."

The words hung in the air. Eren continued, his tone never rising, never falling—a dead man's recitation of facts.

"They've made an alliance with Marley in exchange for our lands. And for our Queen. The plan is to buy time—fifty years of rearmament—using Historia as a breeding sow. Thirteen years of bearing children with royal blood, one after another, until her body gives out or the curse takes her." Eren's hands lay flat on the table, perfectly still. "I'm going to stop them."

Floch processed this with the same numbness he'd cultivated since Shiganshina. Another betrayal. Another death sentence wearing the mask of diplomacy. What did any of it matter?

"And what's the point?" he asked. "Protecting the Queen, saving the island—we're all going to die anyway. You know that as well as I do."

"That's my burden to carry. Not yours."

Something flickered in Floch's chest—not hope, exactly, but something adjacent to it. A recognition. He studied Eren's face, searching for the boy who had once screamed about freedom and killing every last Titan. That boy was gone. In his place sat someone who had looked into the abyss and decided to walk into it anyway.

"If you agree," Eren said, rising from his chair, "come with me. You'll escort Historia to safety."

He extended his hand.

Floch stared at it for a long moment. Then he stood on his own, leaving Eren's hand hanging empty between them.

Something passed across Eren's face—not quite hurt, but close. He lowered his arm and turned toward the door.

Outside, Historia waited in the darkness. Her small frame was rigid with tension, her blue eyes searching Eren's face for answers he clearly wasn't offering.

"We have an arrangement," Eren said without looking at her. "Floch will take you home."

"Just follow me, Your Majesty," Floch said, watching Historia's reaction with new interest.

"Eren." Historia's voice cracked. "Did you tell him everything?"

No response. Eren's silence was its own kind of violence.

"It's not his concern," he said at last.

"How are you going to do this alone?" The desperation in Historia's voice was naked now, raw. "You can't—"

"Follow me, Your Majesty." Floch stepped between them, and something in his tone made Historia stop. "I understand the Queen has lost her political authority. That's what Eren told me, at least."

Eren's expression flickered—nervous, Floch realized. The great Eren Yeager, nervous about what Floch might do.

Floch turned to face him fully. "Relax. I have no interest in using force against women. But it would be better if she doesn't make this difficult."

Their eyes met—Historia's hollow with despair, Eren's carefully blank—and then Floch was guiding the Queen away into the night, leaving Eren standing alone at the farmhouse door.

When Floch glanced back, Eren's lips were moving. Too far to hear the words, but Floch could read the shape of them:

*I'm sorry, Historia. But...*

The rest was lost to darkness.

Floch waited until they were well out of sight. Then he stopped, turned, and seized Historia's arm.

"Now," he said quietly. "Tell me where he's really going."

Historia's eyes—those empty, defeated eyes—suddenly sparked with something alive. Tears spilled down her cheeks, but she was *smiling*, a desperate and grateful thing.

"You're going to help him," she whispered. "You're actually going to help him."

---

The meeting point was a white tent pitched in a forest clearing, invisible from any road. Floch crouched in the shadows and listened.

Inside, Eren spoke with a tall woman whose sharp features and cultured accent marked her as foreign. Yelena, he would later learn—one of the Marleyan Anti-Marleyan Volunteers who had arrived on Paradise's shores with promises and plans.

They were discussing troop movements. Supply lines. The locations of the military's senior leadership.

This was treason. The kind that got you hanged from the walls and left for the crows.

Floch smiled—a real smile, the first in months—and walked into the tent.

Yelena's hand moved toward her weapon. Eren's face went pale.

"Who is this?" Yelena demanded.

"He's—" Eren scrambled for an explanation, caught off-guard for the first time since Floch had known him.

"Apologies for my tardiness." Floch's voice was pleasant, almost bored. "I'm Eren's trusted associate. We can continue."

Later, when Yelena had gone and they were alone, Eren rounded on him.

"I told you to stay with Historia." The words were clipped, barely controlled. "This wasn't part of—"

"Did the Queen order me to come?"

Floch smiled at Eren's frustrated silence. "How could a little girl order me to do anything? Even if she still had her crown, I wouldn't have listened." He stepped closer, close enough to see the tension in Eren's jaw. "I don't follow orders. Not yours. Not anyone's."

"You don't understand what you've done." Eren's voice dropped, dangerous. "There's no going back now. You've signed your own death warrant. And if you tell anyone—"

"Who would I tell?" Floch cut him off. "I don't have any friends."

He let the words settle.

"Unlike you."

Eren said nothing.

"People like you," Floch continued, circling slowly, "always boast about their bonds. Their comrades. Their precious friendships. But when the real problems come, those friends always seem to disappear, don't they?" He paused. "Even that cute blonde boy you chose instead of Commander Erwin."

Still nothing. Eren stood like a statue, his fists clenched at his sides.

"I hope you're really doing this for our homeland." Floch stopped directly in front of him. "But if this is all for your friends—"

"Friends?" Eren's voice was cold enough to freeze blood. "I have no friends."

The words echoed in the empty tent.

Floch studied him for a long moment. Then, surprising himself, he reached out and clasped Eren's shoulder—the gesture of a soldier, a comrade.

"I'll do everything in my power to keep Historia safe," he said. And meant it.

---

**Present**

Floch's eyes snapped open.

The ship swayed beneath him, the sounds of the ocean mixing with distant voices. He was alive. Injured, half-drowned, but alive.

*What the hell is Eren doing?*

The question burned in his mind as he lay motionless, listening. The Alliance—traitors and enemies alike—moved about the deck, planning their insane mission to stop the Rumbling.

*Why hasn't he taken back their Titan powers? Why did he let them escape? Why isn't he stopping them?*

Floch's hand twitched toward where his pistol had been. Gone now, lost to the sea. The powder would have been soaked anyway, useless.

*I have to find another way.*

---

Mikasa sat at the ship's stern, watching Paradise shrink into the distance.

The scarf was wrapped around her neck—Eren's scarf, the one he'd given her when they were children, when he'd saved her life and given her a reason to keep living. She touched the worn fabric, remembering.

---

**One Week Earlier**

The field hospital smelled of blood and antiseptic. Mikasa moved between the cots, checking bandages, offering water, doing the small kindnesses that felt meaningless against so much suffering.

"Mikasa..."

The voice was weak, barely a whisper. Mikasa turned and found Louise—young, earnest Louise who had idolized her since the Battle of Trost—lying pale and still beneath a thin blanket.

She was dying. The wound in her abdomen had been too severe, too deep.

And around her neck, she wore Mikasa's scarf.

No. *Eren's* scarf.

Mikasa knelt beside the cot. Her face betrayed nothing.

"This is your scarf," Louise said, her fingers brushing the red fabric. "Eren... he told me to burn it."

The words hit Mikasa like a physical blow.

*He hates me.*

She had tried to convince herself otherwise. After that horrible conversation in the restaurant—Eren's cruel words, his flat declaration that he had always despised her—she had clung to denial. Jean had assured her that the real Eren would never say such things.

But this was different. This was an order, given privately, with no audience to perform for.

*He really hates me.*

Mikasa sat heavily on the edge of Louise's bed. When she spoke, her voice was rough with something she refused to name.

"Why? Why didn't you burn it?" She turned to look at Louise. "Because you see something special in me?"

Louise's breathing was shallow, labored. "I just... I wanted..."

"When I was a child, Eren saved me." The words spilled out before Mikasa could stop them. "He gave me a family. He gave me this scarf. It was a reminder that we belonged to each other—brother and sister, bound together. I believed that. I thought I was getting stronger to protect my brother."

She stared at her hands.

"But it was all a lie. The Ackerman bloodline... when our power awakens, we imprint on someone. A host. A master. Everything I felt for Eren—all that devotion, all that love—it was never real. It was just... programming."

Louise watched her with fading eyes.

"Eren found out. He told me the truth, made sure I understood that my feelings were fabricated, and then he cut me loose." Mikasa's voice cracked. "I'm sitting here in front of you like a pathetic nobody. Strong because of my blood, not because of anything I earned. Useless to anyone who actually matters."

She touched the scarf again.

"All this time, I never really cared about him. Not the real him. I never asked how he felt, never supported him when he was struggling. And now this scarf means nothing." Her hand fell away. "Because I'm nothing. I was never truly his family."

Louise's trembling fingers found the scarf and began unwinding it from her neck.

"Why?" Mikasa asked.

"Because you want it." Louise's smile was peaceful, almost serene. "Don't you?"

She pressed the fabric into Mikasa's hands.

"Eren told me something else," Louise whispered. "About Zeke. He said... he loves his older brother now. That Zeke is his real family."

Mikasa's throat tightened.

"But then he said..." Louise's voice was fading. "He said he wished he had never learned the truth about the Ackermans. Because he treasured you, Mikasa. He treasured what you had, even if it wasn't real."

---

*"When I learned the truth, I was so disappointed," Eren had said, and Louise had watched the tears slide down his cheeks before he quickly wiped them away. "I thought we were family."*

*He changed his voice, hardened it into something unrecognizable.*

*"Please burn her scarf. If you have the chance."*

---

Mikasa's vision blurred. The tears came without permission, hot and silent.

"How can I fix it?" she asked, though she wasn't sure who she was asking anymore.

A thunderous explosion shook the hospital. Mikasa was at the window in an instant, her soldier's instincts overriding her grief. In the distance, she could see the flashes of ODM gear and thunder spears—a battle between the Yeagerists and someone else.

The Alliance. They were making their move.

She turned back to Louise.

"Hurry," Louise breathed. "Your time has come."

Mikasa crossed the room in three strides and knelt beside the dying girl. She took Louise's hand—the same gesture Captain Levi had made countless times for soldiers who wouldn't see another dawn.

"I swear," Mikasa said, her voice steady now, forged steel over broken glass, "I will protect Eren. No matter the cost. No matter who stands in my way."

She leaned closer.

"Try not to die. And... thank you."

Then she took the scarf, wrapped it around her neck, and ran.

---

**Present**

The memory faded, leaving Mikasa alone with the endless gray sea.

Footsteps approached. She didn't need to look to know who it was—that particular rhythm, that slight hesitation before each step.

Annie Leonhart walked past, heading toward the ship's bow.

Their eyes met.

Mikasa's gaze was cold enough to crack stone. Annie flinched—actually *flinched*—and quickly looked away, continuing on without a word.

*Good*, Mikasa thought. *You should be afraid.*

---

Annie found a quiet spot near the prow and sat with her back against the railing. Her thoughts were a thousand miles away, in a small house in Liberio where an old man with a limp waited for news of his daughter.

*Father...*

"Mind if I join you?"

Armin's voice was gentle, as it always was. Annie didn't respond. She had learned that silence was its own kind of armor.

He sat beside her anyway.

"I've been thinking," he said, staring out at the waves. "About who you remind me of."

"I don't care."

"Eren." Armin continued as if she hadn't spoken. "You remind me of Eren. That same... ruthlessness. That willingness to do whatever it takes." He paused. "I think you might have inspired him to become what he is."

Annie's jaw tightened.

"If we can't stop him peacefully," Armin said quietly, "we'll have to kill him. You understand that, don't you?"

"What?"

"The others—Reiner, Jean, Connie—they're here because they believe peace is possible. They want to believe enemies can become allies." Armin's blue eyes were distant, calculating. "I'm not sure how long that optimism will last. But if Eren refuses to stop, we need a contingency plan. Someone who can strike fast, before the others lose their nerve."

He glanced at Annie.

"The Ackermans are powerful, but they're unreliable. Mikasa is... compromised. Her feelings for Eren make her unpredictable." His gaze was piercing now. "You don't have that problem."

"Find someone else. Talk to Reiner."

"Reiner is broken. You know that as well as I do. He'll hesitate at the crucial moment." Armin leaned closer. "But you—you survived four years in that crystal by thinking of one thing only. Your father. Your reason to live."

Annie's hands curled into fists.

"If you won't help me," Armin said softly, "your father will die with everyone else in Liberio. Is that what you want?"

"You're no different than any of them," Annie spat. "Reiner. Bertholdt. Using people, manipulating them, all while pretending to be kind."

"Maybe." Armin stood, brushing off his pants. "But at least I'm honest about it."

He started to walk away, then paused.

"You kept yourself alive for four years, Annie. Don't tell me it was so you could die now, when your father still needs you."

He left her alone with the weight of his words.

---

Floch had heard everything.

He lay in the shadows near the cargo hold, letting his breaths come shallow and even, playing dead. The Alliance thought him unconscious or close to it. They hadn't bothered to restrain him, hadn't considered that the half-drowned zealot might still be a threat.

*Armin Arlert*, Floch thought. *You've changed since Shiganshina.*

The sweet-faced boy who had once been too frightened to fight—that boy was gone. In his place was something sharper, something that had learned to use kindness as a weapon.

*Maybe I underestimated him. Maybe Eren did too.*

Floch ran through his options. His gun was useless. His body was wrecked. Direct confrontation would be suicide.

*Ingratiate yourself. Become useful. Find the weak points.*

He groaned theatrically and pushed himself up on one elbow.

"I came in peace," he announced to no one in particular.

The response was immediate—weapons drawn, suspicious eyes, the Alliance forming a loose circle around him.

Floch raised his hands slowly, wincing at the pain in his shoulder. "Easy. I'm not looking for a fight."

"Don't shoot!" Armin's voice cut through the tension. He pushed to the front of the group, studying Floch with those unsettlingly intelligent eyes. "What do you want?"

Floch reached for his pistol—slowly, obviously—and held it up for everyone to see. Then he tossed it overboard.

"There," he said. "Happy? The powder was soaked anyway."

"Why are you here?" Armin pressed.

Floch let his shoulders slump, let the weariness show on his face. It wasn't difficult. He *was* exhausted.

"I'm like you," he said. "I want to stop Eren."

Murmurs rippled through the group. Even Mikasa's murderous stare flickered with surprise.

"Why?" Armin asked. "You're his most devoted follower. You founded the Yeagerists in his name."

"Because he demanded it." Floch let bitterness creep into his voice. "He said if I wanted to protect my homeland, I had to build his army. Become his commander. Follow his vision." He shook his head. "But he's gone too far. The Rumbling... I never wanted *this*."

*Lies within lies*, Floch thought. *The best kind.*

"Maybe your approach is worth trying after all," he continued. "The 'humane' option. Because no matter how much faith I had in Eren, he's still..." Floch gestured vaguely. "Weak. Useless without the people around him."

Mikasa's killing intent spiked. Floch noted it and filed it away.

"He never won a single fight on his own merits," Floch pressed. "Lost every battle that mattered. He's nothing without all of you propping him up. So who's to say he can actually pull this off?"

The words were designed to wound, and they did. But they also planted seeds of doubt.

"Enough," Armin said. "If you're sincere, you can help with the plane repairs. But you'll be watched constantly. One wrong move..."

"Understood." Floch gave a mock salute. "So what's the plan? How do you intend to find him?"

Armin hesitated, then seemed to decide that sharing information was worth the risk. "Titans can't swim—they walk on the ocean floor. We left the island later than Eren, but we'll reach the mainland first because the ship is faster than walking. The Rumbling hasn't started yet, which means we might still have time."

"And if we reach him? Then what?"

"We talk. We try to convince him." Armin's expression hardened. "And if that fails... we do what we have to."

Floch nodded, playing the penitent soldier. Inside, his mind was racing.

*I need to sabotage the plane. Or the Alliance itself. Buy Eren time to finish what he started.*

*For the future of Eldia.*

---

The discussion turned to logistics—fuel reserves, flight paths, contingency plans. And then, inevitably, to the two children huddled near the stern.

Gabi Braun and Falco Grice. Warrior candidates. Marleyan soldiers.

*Children*, some part of Floch's mind whispered. But he had stopped thinking of them that way the moment he learned Gabi had killed Sasha Blouse.

"What happens to them?" Reiner asked, nodding toward the pair.

Armin pinched the bridge of his nose. "They stay on the ship. Return to Paradise. We can't bring children into what's coming."

"They can't operate a vessel this size," Hange pointed out. "And we don't know how much fuel is left. They might not make it back."

"That wasn't my idea," Armin said, an edge creeping into his voice. "You brought them. I'm just trying to minimize the damage."

Gabi had been listening. Now she stepped forward, her young face twisted with anger.

"So you're abandoning us? After everything?"

Armin turned to face her fully. His expression was unreadable.

"You think your life is worth more than the millions we're trying to save?"

The question hung in the air like an executioner's blade.

"Leaving you on the ship is *mercy*," Armin continued. "Would you rather come with us into the apocalypse? Die in fire and blood alongside the very people you've spent your whole life learning to hate?"

No one spoke.

"While we stand here debating what to do with two Marleyan soldiers," Armin said quietly, "children are dying. Thousands of them. Crushed beneath Titan feet or burned alive in the chaos. And there are far more of *them* than there are of *you*."

He swept his gaze across the assembled group.

"I'm not your commander. I don't have special knowledge of what's to come. Maybe we save the world. Maybe we all die trying. All I know is that every minute we waste could cost more lives."

He turned back to Gabi.

"You'll be fine. The ship has enough fuel to reach shore, and you're resourceful. You've proven that much." His voice softened slightly. "If one of us survives what's coming, we'll find you."

Then, to the group: "We need to move. Now."

The Alliance began gathering their equipment, preparing for the flight ahead. One by one, they filed past Gabi and Falco—some offering apologetic looks, others avoiding eye contact entirely.

Reiner lingered longest. Gabi reached for him.

"Reiner, wait—"

But he turned away, following the others toward the plane. The weight of his choices pressed down on his shoulders, visible even from a distance.

Gabi's hand fell to her side.

---

Floch watched the exchange from the shadows. When the others had gone, he approached the two children.

"Hey."

They flinched, hands moving toward weapons that weren't there. Floch raised his palms.

"Relax. I'm not here to fight." He studied Falco. "Is it true you can't transform? Can't access your Titan power?"

Falco nodded warily.

Floch considered this. Eren's doing, probably. Some side effect of the Founder's power, or maybe intentional—a way of protecting the kids without revealing his hand.

"I don't understand all the details," Floch said slowly, "but if anyone can help you, it's Eren. He's connected to every Eldian through the paths. If you need something from him..."

He paused, choosing his words carefully.

"Pray. Ask him directly. He'll hear you."

Gabi's eyes widened. "Why would you help us? You're his follower."

"Maybe I am." Floch shrugged. "Or maybe I just don't like seeing kids caught in the middle of someone else's war." He stepped back. "Good luck. You'll need it."

The ship began to recede as the plane lifted off, carrying the Alliance toward their destiny. Floch watched until they became specks against the clouds.

Then he turned back to the deck and began searching for anything that might still be useful.

*Eren. Whatever you're planning... don't fail us.*

---

**Eight Hours Later**

The sea had calmed. Gabi lay in one of the ship's cramped bunks, staring at the ceiling while memories crashed over her like waves.

---

**One Week Earlier**

Paradise Island was burning.

Marleyan soldiers pushed through the streets, their weapons cutting down anyone who stood in their way. Overhead, the Survey Corps fought a desperate battle against the Titans that had somehow appeared within the walls.

And in the chaos, Gabi found herself face to face with a choice.

Sasha Blouse's family—the people who had sheltered her, fed her, shown her kindness despite knowing what she was—huddled behind an overturned cart. Magath's squad advanced toward them, rifles raised.

Gabi's training took over.

Two shots. Both soldiers fell.

Magath spun, clutching his wounded arm, his face contorted with disbelief. "Gabi? What are you—we came here to rescue you!"

"Rescue me?" Gabi reloaded with mechanical precision. "I thought Marley didn't care about Eldian mutts."

"You're different. You're the pride of our—"

"On this terrible island of devils," Gabi cut him off, "I felt something strange. For three days, I lived like a child. No responsibilities. No proving myself. No taking lives to earn the right to exist."

She raised the rifle.

"The demons here live like humans. There are no wars within these walls. You don't have to convince anyone you deserve to be called human."

Magath's hand crept toward his sidearm.

Gabi shot it out of his reach.

"Reiner lived better here," she said quietly. "Before we came and destroyed everything."

Magath's eyes were wild now. "Your parents—they gave you to me—they said—"

"What did they say?"

"That they loved you. That they wanted me to protect you."

"Wrong."

The shot wasn't aimed at Magath. It struck the Titan lumbering toward them, drawing its attention.

"I know who you are, Magath. And I know what my parents really said." Gabi backed away, positioning herself between the Titan and Sasha's family. "Experience the horror the Eldians have lived with for a hundred years."

The Titan's massive hand closed around Magath. His scream was mercifully brief.

Gabi turned to find Kaya staring at her with something like awe.

"Gabi... thank you—"

"Don't." Gabi shoved her away, harder than she'd intended. "Take back your thanks."

"But you saved us—"

"I'm not a person." The words tore out of her like shrapnel. "I'm a weapon. All I know how to do is kill. If you love your sister's memory, don't thank the girl who murdered her."

Kaya's face crumpled.

"She didn't even do anything to me," Gabi whispered. Tears burned tracks down her cheeks. "I just... I killed her for *nothing*."

She snatched up her rifle and ran.

Behind her, Sasha's father called out: "We'll be waiting! Come back when you're ready!"

But Gabi was already gone, swallowed by the smoke and chaos.

---

**Present**

"What are you thinking about?"

Falco's voice pulled her back. He sat on the opposite bunk, watching her with worried eyes.

"What's the point in thinking?" Gabi rolled onto her side. "We're stuck here. There's nothing we can do."

"Were you planning to do something?"

Gabi was quiet for a moment. Then: "They went to stop Eren. Maybe it'll work. Maybe there'll be peace between Paradise and the rest of the world—that's what they're hoping for."

She sat up slowly.

"Or maybe the world will just wipe Paradise off the map. That's possible too. And if that happens..." Her hands clenched. "I won't be able to protect Kaya's family. I can't let that happen."

"And if you *could* stop them? The Alliance?"

"I'd do it." No hesitation. "I'd kill every one of them if it meant protecting the people who showed me kindness."

Falco nodded slowly. "Then I have something to tell you."

"What?"

"I did what Floch suggested. I prayed." He took a breath. "And something answered."

Gabi stared at him.

"I went to... a strange place. Eren was there, and Zeke, and a girl I didn't recognize. She was young, but her eyes were ancient." Falco's voice trembled slightly. "It turns out every Titan Shifter can access that place. The girl—Ymir—she *builds* the Titan bodies."

"Falco, what are you—"

"I told them we needed help. That you were in danger." He met her eyes. "And Eren gave me the power of a flying Titan."

The silence stretched between them.

"You're saying you can transform now."

"Yes."

"Into a *flying* Titan."

"Yes."

Gabi's mind raced. "Can you control it? Is it safe?"

"I think so. The memories are... strange. Mixed up with someone else's. But I can feel the power waiting." Falco reached out and took her hand. "Whatever you decide to do, I'll help you. But I need you to make that decision with *all* the information, not just the parts other people wanted you to know."

"What do you mean?"

"There are things about Eren. About Reiner. About this whole war." Falco's grip tightened. "I know you're tired of being manipulated. Of acting on orders and half-truths. So let me tell you everything I've learned—everything they didn't want us to know."

Gabi searched his face for any sign of deception. She found only earnest, desperate honesty.

"Tell me," she said.

And he did.

He told her about Reiner's breakdown in Liberio. About Eren's conversation with him in that basement—the way Eren had said they were the same, two people who kept moving forward because they had no other choice. About the cycle of hatred that had ground both their peoples down to bone and ash.

He told her about the original attack on Shiganshina, about Marcel's death, about the years Reiner and the others had spent infiltrating the walls. About the guilt that had nearly destroyed Reiner from within.

He told her about Historia, and the plan to turn her into a breeding machine. About the military's betrayal. About Eren's desperate gambit to save his people—and the horrifying cost he was willing to pay.

When he finished, Gabi sat in silence for a long time.

"I'm not going to fight for Eren," she said finally. "Not until I understand him. Not until I know whether he's a monster or something else."

"What are you going to do?"

Gabi stood. Her eyes were clear now, her resolve hardening into something unbreakable.

"I'm going to protect Paradise. Because my *family* is there—the people who took me in, who showed me what it meant to be human." She looked at Falco. "But I'm going to do it my way. Not Marley's way. Not Eren's way."

She extended her hand.

"Are you with me?"

Falco took it without hesitation. "Always."

---

On the deck, under a sky painted with the colors of sunset, Falco transformed.

The Titan that emerged was unlike any Gabi had seen. Wings unfurled from its back—massive, feathered things that caught the wind and held it. The creature's face bore Falco's features, distorted by the transformation but still recognizable.

Still *him*.

Gabi climbed onto its back, settling between the shoulder blades where the feathers grew thickest. Below, the ship dwindled to a toy, then a speck, then nothing.

They rose into the sky, leaving the ocean behind.

Ahead lay an uncertain future—a world on the brink of annihilation, and a girl who had finally learned to fight for something more than approval.

Gabi looked back one last time.

*I don't know what's coming*, she thought. *But I'm done being someone else's weapon.*

*From now on, I fight for myself.*

The wind carried them forward, toward destiny.
